# Legal Basis and Ethical Policy

This document defines what ESM-446 is permitted to do, and why the system is built the way it is.
It is not an afterthought: the constraints below shape the architecture.

## 1. Metadata-only policy

**ESM-446 records signal metadata, never third-party communication content.**

For every detected emission the node stores: UTC timestamp, centre frequency, PMR446 channel index,
received power (dBFS and, when calibrated, dBm), receiver gain configuration, emission duration,
sub-audible CTCSS/DCS code, and estimated SNR.

It does **not** store, transmit, forward, or transcode demodulated audio of third-party
transmissions. Audio demodulation exists in the codebase only for the validation modes described in
§3, which operate on signals generated by the operator or by the built-in simulator.

### Why

Under Spanish law, Art. 39 of *Ley 9/2014 General de Telecomunicaciones* protects the secrecy of
electronic communications; interception and disclosure of third-party content without authorisation
is prohibited, and Art. 197 of the *Código Penal* criminalises it. The equivalent protection at EU
level is Art. 5 of Directive 2002/58/EC (ePrivacy). Merely *listening* to an open PMR446 channel is
not in itself the offence; **recording, storing, and redistributing content is.** The original
version of this project forwarded demodulated audio to a Telegram bot, which crosses that line. That
path has been removed.

### Why it also makes the system better

This is not only a compliance constraint. Metadata is what an ESM (Electronic Support Measures)
system actually produces: an Electronic Order of Battle is built from emitter parameters — frequency,
timing, power, modulation, sub-audible signalling — not from the content of the traffic. Removing the
audio path moved the project *towards* its correct design, not away from it.

## 2. Reception is passive

The node is receive-only. The HackRF One is never placed in transmit mode by any code in this
repository. The 446.0–446.2 MHz PMR446 band is licence-exempt for reception across the EU.

## 3. Test transmissions

Validation requires signals with known ground truth. Three sources are used, in order of preference:

1. **Synthetic IQ** from `esm446.sim` — no RF radiated at all. Covers the majority of the test suite
   and the entire CI pipeline.
2. **Conducted RF** — a signal generator connected to the receiver through coaxial cable and a fixed
   attenuator. Nothing is radiated. This is the method used for power calibration (see
   `docs/04_link_budget.md`), and it is chosen because a known, repeatable cable-plus-attenuator loss
   is a far better calibration reference than an estimated over-the-air path loss.
3. **Radiated test transmissions by the operator**, at minimum power, for end-to-end acceptance
   testing at measured distances.

### Note on the test transmitters

The handheld transceivers used for radiated acceptance testing (§3.3) are amateur-service radios,
not type-approved PMR446 equipment: ETSI EN 300 296 requires an integral antenna and 500 mW ERP,
while these units have a detachable antenna and higher output. Transmitting with them in the PMR446
band is therefore not compliant with the licence-exempt regime, regardless of the band being open.

This is stated plainly rather than glossed over. The mitigations applied: calibration — the
measurement that actually needs signal accuracy — is **conducted, not radiated**; radiated tests are
kept to the transmitter's minimum power setting, brief, and on a channel verified to be clear.
An operator holding an amateur licence should prefer conducting these tests in an amateur allocation
and adjusting the channel plan accordingly.

## 4. Scope of use

This project is published as an engineering and educational portfolio piece: an end-to-end
demonstration of wideband channelisation, constant-false-alarm detection, parameter estimation under
uncertainty, and sensor-to-C2 integration. It is designed for licence-exempt spectrum monitoring and
for signals generated by its own operator.

It is not intended for, and must not be used for, surveillance of individuals, interception of
communication content, or any purpose requiring authorisation the operator does not hold.
